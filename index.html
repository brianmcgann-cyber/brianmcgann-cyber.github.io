Gertie’s building sold to Santora’s Pizza owner, new concept planned for spring

Gertie’s, the popular Clarence Center restaurant, closed this fall, and now the building has a new owner. Paul Santora, owner of Santora’s Pizza, has bought Gertie’s at 6010 Goodrich Road. He plans to launch a new dining concept there in the spring, though he hasn’t decided yet what it will be. The sale was first reported by Buffalo Business First (the story is behind a paywall).

Read their story →

Planning Board approves 50 apartments behind Samuel’s Grande Manor

The Planning Board approved the development plan 6-0 on Wednesday night. The project is 50 units in a three-story building at the back of the property. Town staff said it’s the final board action before construction permits are issued, and the applicant’s attorney, Sean Hopkins, said they hope to break ground in early 2027.

The approval comes with 12 conditions, including dark-sky lighting and screened rooftop equipment. The school district had raised a concern about a nearby playground, and the developer agreed to meet with the district.

Watch the planning board discussion →

Town adopts tax exemption for disabled veterans

After hearing from several veterans and their families, the Town Board voted 5-0 on Wednesday to adopt a town property-tax exemption for veterans who are permanently and totally disabled as a result of military service. It takes effect March 1st, 2027.

The Clarence School District is weighing a matching school-tax exemption. The school board expects to decide at its January 11th meeting, and the district’s public comment period is open through December 16th.

Watch the town meeting → School district comment info →

Development roundup: Transit Road, Lapp Road, and Clarence Center Road

PB Investors, 5695 & 5731 Transit Road: The Planning Board denied a concept plan to raise this already-approved project from 84 to 94 apartments. The developer asked for the denial: the site has no allowable density left, so a denial is the required step before seeking a variance from the Zoning Board of Appeals. If the ZBA grants it, the plan comes back to the Planning Board. Watch the meeting →

Lapp Road: The Town Board sent a proposed 9-lot subdivision on 36 vacant acres on the south side of Lapp Road, west of Heise Road, to the Planning Board for review. Four lots would be about 1.4 to 1.9 acres and five would be 5 acres or more. Watch the meeting →

Clarence Center United Methodist Church, 9750 Clarence Center Road: The Planning Board approved splitting off a vacant lot of about 9.3 acres, which the church plans to sell to help pay for upkeep of its 30-year-old building. A church representative said a deed restriction will allow only single-family homes. Watch the meeting →

Clarence Center’s Gavin McCarthy making a push for the Sabres

Gavin McCarthy, a 21-year-old defenseman who grew up in Clarence Center, is turning heads in his first full season with the Rochester Americans. The former Boston University captain, a Sabres third-round pick in 2023 (86th overall), has six points (one goal, five assists) in 13 games. With the Sabres’ third defensive pair struggling, a call-up wouldn’t be a surprise, though it may not be imminent.

How do salaries in Clarence Schools compare to other districts?

The Empire Center released “Educator Pay 2026” this week, comparing school salaries across New York. Statewide, 114 school employees are paid over $300,000, the average full-time employee earns $99,652, and the highest-paid school employee makes $437,000. Averages for Clarence employees are well below the state average, and our superintendent’s salary is less than that of 17 other superintendents in Western New York. Individual school employee salaries are public record.

Defense attorneys claim Clarence man “accidentally” shot and killed his wife

Michael Brown of Clarence was indicted on June 18th in the death of his wife. This week, his attorneys claimed the shooting was accidental. They also claim Brown was too intoxicated to speak voluntarily with investigators or to waive his Miranda rights. The next court date is scheduled for January.

Town adopts 2027 budget of $32.76 million

The Clarence town budget for 2027 was adopted at a public hearing on November 4th, where no town residents spoke. The budget is set at $32,760,000, up from $30,698,000 in 2026. The general fund tax rate remains unchanged, while spending rises in areas like fuel, road salt, utilities, employee raises and senior center funding.

The Clarence Varsity Soccer team won the Section VI Championship by defeating Williamsville North on October 31st. The team advanced to the New York State Public High School Athletic Association finals against Churchville-Chili on Saturday, November 7th.
